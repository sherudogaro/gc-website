Risk Hiding in Plain Sight

Clients started sending us retirement accounts they held elsewhere and asking what was in them. We looked at a lot of them, because the first few were concerning enough that we wanted to know whether they were unusual. They weren't.

Take somebody around fifty, retiring in 2040. What they typically get is a target-date fund holding four things. Now look underneath it.

What the plan holds Weight What it is really exposed to
US equities ~45% AI concentration
International equities ~21% AI concentration, via the supply chain
Bonds ~25% Sovereign debt repricing
Private equity & private credit ~9% Impaired tech-adjacent credit

The US sleeve is an index fund owning thousands of companies, and it's index tracking, so what you're getting when it's all said and done is the S&P 500. The usual suspects. Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon. The top eight names are about 30% of it. You'd think the international sleeve gets you away from that. Its four largest holdings are Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung, ASML and SK hynix, which are the companies manufacturing the chips the whole AI buildout runs on.

The private equity slice was meant to diversify away from equities, and a lot of that money is now lending to AI firms. It isn't achieving anything either. It's just adding risk. And the bonds, the part that's supposed to be safe, are the pivotal market, because sovereign bonds reprice everything else. These AI companies have taken on serious debt. Rates go higher, their cost of capital goes higher, and that impairs the equity.

People think they're well diversified because they hold four things. The correlations are a lot closer to one than that. Anyone who sat through 2022, when bonds and equities went down together, has seen a smaller version of it, and there's more leverage in the market now than there was then.

The other thing worth noticing is what these plans don't own. In the one we went through, energy was about 4% and basic materials about 4%. Minuscule.

That example is a US plan, because it's what most of the statements we were sent looked like. If your pension is held in the UK, Australia, New Zealand or Europe, the exposures are much the same, because these plans are all built out of the same handful of global index funds. If anything it's more direct, because the global fund that was supposed to be your diversified option has the US market as its largest single exposure. And the bond problem isn't a US-only trade either.
